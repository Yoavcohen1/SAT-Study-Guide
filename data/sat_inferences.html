Inferences

Inference questions ask you to choose what must be true based on the text. The answer is not always stated directly, but it must be supported by the passage.

LAMP Strategy

Last Sentence

Assess
Look back to get context.
Make a prediction
Predict the missing idea.
Purge choices
Eliminate answers that do not fit.

Start near the end

The final sentence often creates the gap you need to fill.

Stay supported

Inference Example

Marine biologists studying a species of deep-sea octopus noticed that the animals gathered near warm water vents on the ocean floor. The vents created unusually warm conditions compared with the surrounding deep-sea environment. Eggs laid near the vents developed much faster than eggs laid in colder areas. Because faster development reduces the amount of time eggs are exposed to predators, researchers believe the octopuses may be choosing nesting sites that improve their offspring's chances of survival.

Question: Which choice most logically follows from the text?

A. Deep-sea octopuses avoid laying eggs near warm water vents because the vents attract predators.
BWarm water vents may make some nesting sites more beneficial for deep-sea octopuses than colder areas are.
C. Eggs laid in colder areas always fail to develop into adult octopuses.
D. Deep-sea octopuses are the only animals that use warm water vents for nesting.

Last sentence

Researchers think the octopuses choose sites that improve survival.

Context

Warm vents help eggs develop faster, which reduces exposure to predators.

Prediction

Warm vents are probably helpful nesting locations.

Correct choice

B matches that prediction without adding unsupported claims.

Detail-Based Inference

Archaeologists studying an ancient settlement found several storage jars containing traces of wheat, barley, and lentils. The jars were located in a central building rather than in individual homes. The building also contained tools used to measure and divide grain. These findings suggest that the settlement's food supply may have been managed collectively rather than separately by each household.

Question: Which choice is best supported by the text?

A. Each household in the settlement grew only one type of crop.
B. The settlement's residents did not know how to store grain safely.
CSome food resources in the settlement may have been stored and distributed from a shared location.
D. The central building was used mainly as a place for religious ceremonies.

Common inference traps

  • Too strong: words like always, never, only, or must can make an answer too extreme.
  • Too broad: the answer goes beyond what the text supports.
  • Topic match only: the answer mentions the same topic but does not follow from the passage.
  • Opposite direction: the answer reverses what the evidence suggests.
