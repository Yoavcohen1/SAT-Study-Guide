Standard English Conventions

Boundaries

Boundaries questions test how to punctuate between sentences and clauses. Find where one complete idea ends and the next begins, then choose the punctuation that fits.

Priority List

When more than one choice seems possible, work through the options in this order.

  1. ColonUse it first when an independent clause sets up an expanding thought.
  2. Period, Semicolon, Comma + FANBOYSThe three ways to join two independent clauses.
  3. Isolated CommaFor a list or to attach a non-essential element.
  4. No punctuationWhen nothing belongs between the words.

Punctuation Process

  1. Look at the answer choices first.
  2. Plan out all your steps just from the answer choices.
  3. Find what you need to look for in the passage.
  4. Look for that in the passage.
  5. Look for the subject and the main verb.
  6. Comma jumping: start at the beginning of the sentence and stop at the next comma — that is where the inserted phrase ends.
  7. Make sure you fully understand the structure of the sentence before you select the answer.

All Punctuations

PunctuationWhen to use itExample
Colon1 thing Independent clause before, and an expanding thought after (a list or explanation). They had one goal: safety.
Period1 thing Independent clause before and independent clause after. The bridge was old. Engineers replaced it.
Semicolon2 things 1. A semicolon list (separates items that already contain commas).
2. Independent clause before and independent clause after.		 We visited Paris, France; Rome, Italy; and Bern, Switzerland.

The bridge was old; engineers replaced it.
Comma + FANBOYS1 thing Independent clause before and independent clause after, joined by for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so. The bridge was old, so engineers replaced it.
Isolated Comma2 things 1. A comma list.
2. Attaches a non-essential element.		 We bought apples, pears, and figs.

The bridge, built in 1910, was replaced.
Dash2 things 1. Attaches a non-essential element (must be a dash sandwich — a matching pair).
2. Same thing as a colon.		 The bridge — built in 1910 — was replaced.

They had one goal — safety.

How to avoid traps

Two complete sentences

You may not join them with a comma alone — that is a comma splice. Use a period, semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS.

Colon test

The words before a colon must form a complete sentence that sets up what follows.

Dash sandwich

A dash that sets off extra information needs a matching dash on the other side.

Subject and verb

Find them first — they tell you where each complete idea begins and ends.
