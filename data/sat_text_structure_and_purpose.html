How to Think About These Questions

There are three common types of text structure and purpose questions. Each one asks you to describe what the passage or a specific sentence is doing.

Main function of the text

These are basically main idea questions in different wording. Read the passage, notice what each line contributes, then create a main idea in your head before checking the answer choices.

Function of a specific line

Go back to the underlined line and ask what that line only contributes. Does it present a generalization, hypothesis, conclusion, example, contrast, or setup? Try labeling the sentence in two or three words.

Overall structure

Find the role of each sentence or section and the order in which they appear. The correct answer should match both the roles and the sequence.

Avoid answers that are

  • incorrect according to the text
  • outside the scope of the text or based on an unsupported assumption
  • too extreme compared with what the passage actually says

Quick test

Can you label the sentence in two or three words? Example: gives example, introduces contrast, sets up description, states conclusion.

Answer trap

Wrong answers often sound related to the topic, but they describe the wrong role or the wrong order.
