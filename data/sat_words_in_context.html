The following text is adapted from Nathaniel Hawthorne's 1837 story "Dr. Heidegger's Experiment." The main character, a physician, is experimenting with rehydrating a dried flower.

At first [the rose] lay lightly on the surface of the fluid, appearing to imbibe none of its moisture. Soon, however, a singular change began to be visible. The crushed and dried petals stirred and assumed a deepening tinge of crimson, as if the flower were reviving from a deathlike slumber. dramatic

Question: As used in the text, what does the phrase "a singular" most nearly mean?

A. A lonely
B. A disagreeable
C. An acceptable
D An extraordinary

How to Think About These Questions

Do not use the modern meaning only

SAT vocabulary questions often use familiar words in less common ways. The sentence decides the meaning.

Look around the word

Read the sentence before and after the word. In this example, the rose changes dramatically and seems to revive, so "singular" means unusual or extraordinary.

Predict before checking options

Write a quick meaning in your head. Then choose the answer that best matches it.

Avoid answers that are

  • based only on a common meaning of the word
  • too negative or too positive for the tone
  • not supported by the surrounding sentence
