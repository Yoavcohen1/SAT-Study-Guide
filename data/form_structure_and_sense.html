Standard English Conventions

Form, Structure and Sense

These grammar questions test four must-know patterns: verbs, pronouns, modifiers, and apostrophes. Learn the rules below and you will recognize them instantly.

The Four Patterns

Verbs

Match the verb to its real subject; ignore words in between.

Pronouns

Find the antecedent, then match number and case.

Modifiers

The word right after the comma must be what the phrase describes.

Apostrophes

Possession or contraction — never just a plural.

2-step pronoun process: ① Find the antecedent (the noun the pronoun replaces). ② Match the pronoun to it.
Arcata is one of the coolest places in California. However, many people don't even know it exists. — "it" stands for the singular "Arcata."

Verb Agreement

Her exhaustive chromosomal survey of plants in Silent Valley, a tropical forest home to nearly 1,000 species of native flora (many of which are endangered), instrumental in the government's decision to preserve the forest. subject = survey (singular)

Question: Which choice conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. are
B. were
C. have been
Dwas
Cross out the long phrase between the subject and the verb. The subject is the singular noun survey, so it takes the singular verb was.

Modifiers

The rule: whatever is described before the comma must come right after the comma.

Far from being modern inventions, more than 5,000 years ago. what isn't modern? the straws

Question: Which choice conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. Sumerians in ancient Mesopotamia used drinking straws
Bdrinking straws were used by Sumerians in ancient Mesopotamia
C. the use of drinking straws by Sumerians happened
D. ancient Mesopotamia was home to Sumerians who used drinking straws

Apostrophes

Apostrophes show possession (the Moon's pull) or form contractions (it's = it is). A plain plural takes no apostrophe.

Earth is not a perfect sphere. Due to the gravitational pull, Earth bulges out on the sides closest to and farthest from the Moon. one Moon, owns the pull

Question: Which choice conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

AMoon's
B. Moons
C. Moons'
D. Moon

Confusables the SAT loves

its vs it's

its = belonging to it; it's = it is.

there / their / they're

place; possession; they are.

who's vs whose

who's = who is; whose = possession.

singular vs plural verb

Cross out phrases between the subject and verb, then check agreement.
