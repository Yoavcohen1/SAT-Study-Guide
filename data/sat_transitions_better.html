Expression of Ideas

Transitions

Transition questions test whether you understand the relationship between two ideas. Do not choose by vibe. Read around the blank, name the relationship, then pick the matching transition.

SLAP Strategy

Eliminate choices that do not match your prediction.

Transition Types

Transition Type Transition Words Sample Sentence Use
Additions or ContinuationsAdding similar information Additionally, furthermore, in addition, moreover, also The company implemented new training programs. Additionally, it updated its safety protocols. Mostly interchangeable
ComparisonsSimilarity or parallel ideas Likewise, similarly The museum expanded its exhibits to attract new visitors. Likewise, the library increased its community outreach. Mostly interchangeable
ExamplesSpecific example or instance For example, for instance Many species are endangered due to habitat loss. For example, the Amur leopard has seen a sharp decline. Interchangeable
Specific EmphasisHighlighting a particular aspect Specifically, in particular Pollution affects many habitats. Specifically, coral reefs are at risk due to rising ocean temperatures. Different emphasis
Clarification or RephrasingRestating for clarity In other words, that is, that is to say Her popularity was nonexistent. In other words, nobody liked her. Some cases
Summative ConclusionsConcluding or summarizing In sum, in summary The process requires research, planning, and revision. In summary, it takes time. Interchangeable
Contrasts or ConcessionsCounterpoint or exception However, nevertheless, nonetheless, still, granted, that being said, admittedly, regardless He wanted to invest in tech stocks. However, his advisor recommended safer options. Many cases
Alternatives or OptionsDifferent perspective or choice On the other hand, in contrast, by contrast, conversely, alternatively, instead The company could expand its workforce. On the other hand, it could train existing employees. Specific use
Sequence or OrderOrder or progression First, next, then, subsequently, finally, ultimately, secondly First, preheat the oven. Next, mix the ingredients. Specific step
Cause and Effect - Direct ResultExplaining an outcome As a result, consequently, therefore, thus, hence The team missed the deadline. As a result, they had to work overtime. Many cases
Cause and Effect - PurposeGoal-oriented reason For this reason, accordingly, to that end The project required more resources. For this reason, the team requested funding. Many cases
Emphasis or IntensificationHighlighting importance In fact, indeed Her work has been groundbreaking. In fact, she recently won an award. Many cases
Time ReferencesTiming or temporal shifts Earlier, previously, meanwhile, today, currently, later, nowadays The company struggled last year. Today, it has expanded its workforce. Specific time
Context-Based TransitionsFlexible based on sentence context Actually, elsewhere, in many cases, in reality, in turn, fittingly The policy may be challenging to implement. Actually, it requires extensive training. Flexible use

Practice Example

Example

Some researchers argue that urban gardens mainly improve neighborhood appearance. , recent studies suggest that these gardens can also reduce local temperatures and improve access to fresh food.

Question: Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A. Therefore,
BHowever,
C. For example,
D. Similarly,

Why B works

Idea before the blank

Urban gardens improve appearance.

Idea after the blank

Urban gardens also have practical benefits. This expands and partly shifts the first idea.

Relationship

The second idea introduces a contrast or correction to a limited view.

Best transition

However fits because it signals a shift from the first claim to a broader view.
